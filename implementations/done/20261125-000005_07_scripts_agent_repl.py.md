## Goal

## Assumptions

- `_persist_session_state()` (`scripts/agent/repl.py:94-102`) already feeds one snapshot to both persist calls, so the gap this row closes is that the call sits outside the coordinator with no deadline and no step record, not that two snapshots are taken. Verified.
- The coordinator is built in `__init__` before the orchestrator exists, which is why `register_close_hook` at `scripts/agent/repl.py:136-143` is the post-startup registration point. The persist step is NOT registered at that point: the startup-failure path reaches the `finally` before any post-startup registration runs, and it persisted before this row, so registering the step post-startup would silently turn that path into `not_started`. The step is registered in `__init__`, where the persister already exists.
- `SignalHandler` (`scripts/agent/signal_handler.py`) has no budget parameter yet: SIGINT arming is row 10 (R6). This row hands the budget to the coordinator and the input loop only.
- The tracked writer this row registers is the workflow `StateStore`, and it is private to `Orchestrator` (`scripts/agent/orchestrator.py:94`, closed by `close()` at `:243-245`). `AppServices` (`scripts/agent/context.py:326-345`) does not carry it, and `orchestrator.py` is not in the Plan's target-file table. ## Design decisions

- Persistence becomes a coordinator step rather than staying a `finally` call: only the coordinator can give it a deadline and a record, and a step that fails is reported rather than printed as a warning from the REPL.
- The `finally` keeps exactly one call: `close_resources()`. Removing the persist call from it is what makes the sequence single-owner (REQ-001).
- The budget is constructed here, unarmed, and handed to the coordinator and the input loop, so both consumers read one object (Plan Design, "One budget, many consumers"). `SignalHandler` joins them in row 10.
- The persist step is registered in `__init__` rather than at the post-startup hook site, because the startup-failure path must keep persisting (see Assumptions).
- Compatibility impact: the shutdown output changes (a persist step record replaces the `Session persistence failed` warning path), which row 32 asserts.

## Alternatives considered

- keep the persist call in the `finally` and wrap it in a timeout — rejected: it would still run outside the sequence, so its time would not be counted against the deadline and its outcome would not appear in the report.

## Implementation
### Target file
`scripts/agent/repl.py`

### Procedure
1. Construct the budget in `__init__` from `AgentConfig.shutdown_deadline_seconds` (row 17) and pass it to `ResourceShutdownCoordinator` and `ReplInputLoop` as the keyword-only `budget` argument. `SignalHandler` is not given it in this row (row 10 adds that parameter).
2. Register the snapshot-persist step with the coordinator in `__init__`, not at the post-startup hook site: the startup-failure path reaches the `finally` before any post-startup registration runs, and it persisted before this row.
3. Register the tracked writer at the post-startup hook site, next to `register_close_hook("workflow_store", ...)`, through the new `Orchestrator.state_store` property and `StateStore.get_connection()`.
4. Delete the `_persist_session_state()` call and its warning handler from the `finally` (`scripts/agent/repl.py:157-171`), leaving `close_resources()` as the only shutdown call.
5. Keep `_persist_session_state()` as the registered step body and give it an optional `window` (the seconds the coordinator says the step may take), converted into one monotonic `deadline` passed to both persist calls (row 9). Do not inline a lambda: the named method is the existing test seam (`tests/agent/test_repl.py:115`, `:143`) and keeps the one-snapshot docstring.

### Method
- The step callable captures one snapshot and passes it to both persist calls, so the snapshot the step records and the snapshot the persister writes are the same object (REQ-007).
- The budget is one object shared by the input loop and the coordinator; the coordinator arms it in `enter_shutdown`, so the REPL constructs it unarmed.

### Details
- Registering in `__init__` is earlier than `bind()`, so every exit path that reaches the `finally` already carries the step, which is the property the comment at `scripts/agent/repl.py:136-143` protects.
- The registered step is the bound method captured at construction, so a test that reassigns `repl._persist_session_state` after construction no longer intercepts the step. Companion row 27 moves those ordering assertions to the coordinator's step records.

## Compatibility considerations

- Exit paths that previously persisted without a deadline now persist inside the budget; a persist step that cannot start is recorded `not_started` (row 5) instead of running unbounded.
- The `Session persistence failed` warning line disappears from the REPL output; the step record carries the outcome instead (row 32).
- `tests/agent/test_repl.py::_make_real_wired_repl` patches `AgentContext` with a plain `MagicMock`, so `cfg.shutdown_deadline_seconds` is a mock and `ShutdownBudget` rejects it with `TypeError`. Companion row 27 gives that fake cfg a numeric deadline; the production path always has a float.
- `tests/agent/test_repl.py::TestRunResourceCleanup` spies `repl._persist_session_state` after construction and asserts the `finally` call order; both are obsolete under the step machine and are rewritten by row 27 against the coordinator's step records.
