vocabulary (Finding / Candidate / Proposal / Approved Change / Blocked / Not

## Background
The current `prompts/04_refactor.md` workflow (Steps 0-10) protects behavior during a
refactor (Behavior Lock, exception/API freeze, mutation testing) but has no structured
way to record a discovered problem — e.g. duplicate validation logic or an
over-concentrated responsibility — separately from the approved change scope. Today,
the only related mechanism is the Step 2 / Step 10 "Proposal" concept, which exists
solely for *behavior-changing ideas surfaced while working on the approved target
file*; it has no vocabulary for *investigation-time findings that are evidence-based
but not yet evaluated for action*.

Provenance note: this issue (`issues/20260826_01_issue.md`) and its four siblings

## Problem
`prompts/04_refactor.md` conflates two different activities during a refactor:
(a) safely transforming the approved target file, and (b) noticing — but not acting
on — unrelated technical debt or responsibility problems encountered while reading it.
Today there is no reporting channel for (b) other than the narrower, behavior-change-
specific "Proposal" mechanism, so an AI running this workflow has no sanctioned way to
surface a duplicate-validation or unclear-ownership observation without either staying
silent about it or overstepping into unapproved implementation.

## Reason for change
Without a defined discovery vocabulary, a real technical-debt observation made during
a refactor either gets dropped (undocumented) or risks being implemented outside the
approved scope. A structured, evidence-required Finding format closes this gap while
keeping the existing safety guarantees (Behavior Lock, API/exception freeze, Completion
gate) fully intact.
