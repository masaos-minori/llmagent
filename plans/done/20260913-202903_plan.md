`docs/03_rag_02_03_ingestion_pipeline-chunksplitter.md`, and renumber the
subsequent, non-duplicate "ChunkSplitter" continuation section (`## 3b.`) to restore
a clean, sequential section numbering.

## Background
The Issue's cited line range (100-174) for the duplicate section is accurate. Direct
inspection confirms `docs/03_rag_02_03_ingestion_pipeline-chunksplitter.md` contains
three separately-headed "ChunkSplitter" sections, each preceded by a repeated
"# RAG Ingestion Pipeline" nav block and followed by its own `## Related Documents`/
`## Keywords` sub-sections:
- `## 3. ChunkSplitter` (line 32-99)
- `## 3a. ChunkSplitter` (line 107-174)
- `## 3b. ChunkSplitter` (line 182-324)

**Re-verified 2026-09-13** (this cycle): a line-by-line `diff` between section `3`
(lines 32-99) and section `3a` (lines 107-174) shows only 4 differing lines total —
all of them the section-heading text itself (`## 3.` vs `## 3a.`); the body content
(Class Overview, Module-level Constants, TypedDicts, config table) is byte-for-byte
identical. This confirms the Issue's claim exactly: section `3a` is an accidental
full duplicate of section `3`.

However, the Issue's Recommended Action ("Remove the duplicate section (lines
100-174) and retain only the first instance") does not account for a third section
the Issue did not examine: `## 3b. ChunkSplitter` (lines 182-324) is **not** a
duplicate — a `diff` against section `3` shows substantial content differences
(section `3b` begins at sub-section `### 3.1.3 Markdown Source Detection Behavior`,
covering Markdown-heading chunking behavior and splitting-strategy details that
section `3`/`3a` do not contain at all). Section `3b` is a genuine continuation of
the ChunkSplitter documentation, not a duplicate, and must be preserved — simply
deleting "everything after the first instance" as the Issue's Recommended Action
literally states would destroy this unique content.

## Problem
1. Section `3a` (lines 100-174, including its repeated nav-header block and
   `Related Documents`/`Keywords` sub-sections) is a byte-for-byte duplicate of
   section `3` (lines 32-99), doubling maintenance burden and creating confusion for
   readers who encounter the same content twice
2. After removing section `3a`, the surviving section `3b` would leave a numbering
   gap (`3`, then `3b`, with no `3a`) unless renumbered

## Reason for change
Remove the accidental duplicate to eliminate redundant content and the maintenance
burden of keeping two copies in sync, while preserving the genuinely distinct
continuation content in the (renumbered) surviving section.

## Implementation intent
1. Delete lines 100-174 (the second "# RAG Ingestion Pipeline" nav block through the
   end of section `3a`'s `## Keywords` sub-section) in their entirety
2. Rename the heading `## 3b. ChunkSplitter (...)` (now immediately following section
   `3`'s content after the deletion) to `## 3a. ChunkSplitter (...)`, restoring
   sequential numbering (`3`, `3a`)
3. Do not alter any other content in either surviving section

## Design
This task is classified as **Path A** (small task): affects 1 file, no public
interface changes, no database schema changes, and no source code changes at all
(documentation-only).

This cycle's re-verification confirmed the Issue's core claim (sections `3`/`3a` are
byte-for-byte duplicates) but found the Issue's proposed fix incomplete: it did not
account for section `3b`, a genuine continuation (not a duplicate) that must be
preserved and renumbered rather than deleted along with `3a`.

Key design decisions:
1. Renumber `3b` → `3a` (rather than leaving a `3`/`3b` gap, or renumbering to some
   other scheme) — this is the minimal change that restores sequential numbering
   without needing to renumber `3b`'s own internal sub-section numbers
   (`3.1.3`, `3.1.4`, `3.2`, ...), which remain valid as sub-numbers of the section
   now labeled `3a`
2. Delete the entire repeated nav-header block (the second "# RAG Ingestion
   Pipeline" + links + `---`) along with the duplicate section body, not just the
   `## 3a.` heading and its immediate content — leaving a stray, unlabeled nav block
   would itself be a documentation defect
